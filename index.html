The plot,so far

Four chapters · two minutes
01The family firm

For a long time I worked alongside my father at our graphic design firm. The funny part? I was a designer wasn't a designer. But I was doing pretty well at it.

Four years, 50+ clients, websites and brand identities for the likes of Karisyo Events and Qbees Lounge, a long-running marketing partnership with Cake Box, and 100+ brochures later, I'd learnt something that mattered more than Photoshop.

02The realisation

Clients weren't losing sleep over the technical brilliance of a design. They cared about three things.

01Whatthey needed
02Whythey needed it
03Whenthey'd get it
04Kerningsaid no client, ever

So instead of trying to become a designer, I focused on understanding requirements, developing concepts and themes, and streamlining the process so our designers could bring ideas to life faster.

03The lesson
Still true, still on the fridge

The best solution isn't always the most sophisticated one. It's the one that solves the right problem.

04Plot twist

That curiosity led me to a Master's in Data Science, and to questions that intuition alone can't answer.

Since then I've modelled 402,005 used-car listings, chased player-minutes patterns in football, and experimented with machine learning, statistics and data-driven decisions. I'm more confident than ever about understanding the problem first. Problems at the intersection of data, business and human behaviour excite me the most.

I still can't draw a straight line. Luckily, linear regression can.

How I work

The three questions from the print shop. They work on data problems too.
